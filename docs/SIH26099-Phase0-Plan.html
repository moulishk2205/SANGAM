The four findings that change the plan

1 · The data problem is solved, and better than you hoped

The US Defense Logistics Agency publishes the complete FLIS / PUB LOG catalogue as free monthly CSVs, no login. It contains ~80,000 approved item names with definitions, a 27,552-row attribute dictionary, controlled vocabularies, and — decisively — two real ground-truth relations for exactly your problem: many manufacturer part numbers mapping to one canonical item (V_FLIS_PART), and human standardization decisions recording that item X replaces item Y (V_FLIS_STANDARDIZATION).

You do not have to invent your labels. You can report precision and recall against decisions made by a real national codification authority. Everything synthetic is then only the surface text — the messy way five CPSEs would each write down the same real item.

2 · Your §5 data assumptions are factually wrong

"Public government procurement datasets, public CPSE material catalogs, GeM data where legally usable" — none of these yield item-level material data that is both accessible and legal. VERIFIED data.gov.in has zero item-level material records. GeM's own website policy forbids reproduction of its content. The CPPP tender portal is CAPTCHA-gated. Commercial catalogues (McMaster, Fastenal, IndiaMART) block crawlers in robots.txt.

Correct this before the presentation. A judge from a CPSE will know it is wrong, and "we scraped GeM" in front of a Government of India panel ends your slot.

3 · Semantic similarity must never decide identity

You identified this correctly but drew the wrong architectural conclusion. Your pipeline treats "Hybrid Scoring" as a blend of a similarity number and an attribute number. It must instead be: retrieval by text, decision by attribute logic, with a mismatch on a discriminating attribute acting as a hard veto — not a score penalty.

This is backed by a verified admission from the authors of DITTO, the leading deep entity-matching system: "the LMs that we have tested in Ditto are pre-trained on general English text corpora and thus might not capture well EM tasks with a lot of numeric data." Put that quote on a slide. M10×50 vs M10×75 must be structurally impossible to call a duplicate, not merely unlikely.

4 · The brief is missing the strongest identity signal in the domain

There is no manufacturer part number, no drawing number, and no revision anywhere in your 36 sections. In real MRO masters these beat the description outright. The NATO Codification System formalises this as the reference method of identification, and real Indian CPSE data confirms it — a live BHEL Haridwar tender contains rows like DRG: 0105255634 1 REV: 04 PISTON RING MATERIAL: COCR20W15NI.

Add both matchers and fuse them. A normalised-MPN match across two CPSEs is near-proof of identity, and it produces a spectacular demo moment: it catches duplicates whose descriptions share almost no words.

What we recommend you build

A codification bureau, not a deduplication tool. Copy the NCS model explicitly: an approved item-name dictionary, per-name attribute templates, stable attribute codes, a non-significant national identifier, and dual reference/descriptive identification. India already runs a NATO-compliant codification bureau for defence materiel through NCB (India); there is no civil-CPSE equivalent. That gap is real, checkable, and it is your pitch.

Why duplicates actually arise

Worth stating in your deck, because it motivates the prevention feature and shows you understand the cause rather than the symptom.

  1. The 40-character ceiling. MAKT-MAKTX is CHAR 40 on domain TEXT40 and SAP does not support lengthening it. Every abbreviation catastrophe in your brief exists because a storekeeper had 40 characters and a full specification to express. Different sites abbreviate differently, drop different words, and reorder differently.
  2. Search that cannot find what exists. SAP's standard material search is a LIKE on that 40-char string. If you cannot find BLT HEX by typing BOLT, you create a new code. Duplicate creation is a search failure.
  3. Decentralised creation — each plant has code-creation rights, under procurement time pressure.
  4. Legacy migration — multiple systems merged into one SAP client. MARA-BISMT (old material number) is the fossil record.
  5. Vendor-driven descriptions — copied verbatim from whichever vendor quoted first.
  6. Unit-of-measure ambiguity — stocked as EA at one plant, BOX-100 at another.

Why one database is a pitchable feature, not a shortcut

Every retrieval in this system is vector similarity ∧ tenant ∧ category ∧ approval-status — a relational query with a vector predicate. Splitting vectors into a separate store means either dual-write reconciliation or a network round-trip back into Postgres, to buy latency you do not need. You get transactional consistency between a material, its embedding, its approval and its audit row. "We have no eventual-consistency window between an approval and the record it approves" is a real enterprise answer to a CIO.

Write the crossover point into the doc so it reads as costed rather than lazy: "We migrate to a dedicated ANN store when >10M vectors, or p95 retrieval exceeds 200 ms at recall@50 ≥ 0.95, or HNSW rebuild exceeds the maintenance window. None hold at national-pilot scale; all are monitored."

Your 14-step pipeline is roughly right in sequence and wrong in emphasis. It puts the difficulty in "Hybrid Scoring" when the difficulty is in extraction, and it treats attribute comparison as a scoring input when it must be a gate.

The recommended engine

01
Ingest & field split
Blob descriptions carry embedded structure. Pull out DRG:, REV:, MATERIAL:, MAKE:, legacy codes and vendor suffixes before anything else. Keep the raw row immutable forever.
02
Normalise
Case, punctuation, separator unification (X/x/*/×/-), whitespace, then gazetteer abbreviation expansion. The gazetteer is LLM-bootstrapped, human-approved, versioned, and is a first-class reviewable artefact — not model-internal knowledge.
03
Extract attributes — three-layer cascade
(a) grammar/regex for dimensions, threads, units, standards codes, grades — high precision, deterministic, confidence 1.0, emits a rule ID. (b) gazetteer + fuzzy for noun, modifier, material family. (c) small BIO tagger for the residual only, trained on LLM-bootstrapped labels. Every value carries provenance: rule:R17 / gaz:v3 / model:v1.2@0.83.
04
Assign approved item name (INC)
Classification into the item-name dictionary. This is what selects the attribute template and therefore which attributes are decisive for this item type. Embedding kNN over the 79,883 FLIS item-name definitions, gated by a gazetteer of noun heads.
05
Canonicalise
Pint converts every quantity to canonical units. Emit the canonical spec key, the full canonical description, and a ≤40-char SAP-compatible short text. That last one nobody else at SIH will think of.
06
Reference match (runs first)
Normalised manufacturer + normalised MPN, or drawing number + revision. Exact match here is near-proof of identity and bypasses the descriptive path. Trap: same drawing number with different revisions is not identity.
07
Retrieve candidates — three channels, RRF-fused
(a) HNSW over fine-tuned bge-base-en-v1.5 of the canonical string. (b) BM25 over a spec-aware tokenizer emitting thread=M10, len_mm=50 as atomic tokens plus char 3–4-grams. (c) exact canonical-spec-key lookup. Fuse with RRF, k=60. Metric that matters: pair completeness, which caps everything downstream.
08
Compare attributes — three-valued
Per attribute: AGREE / DISAGREE / UNKNOWN, with a per-attribute tolerance policy. Missing ≠ mismatched. UNKNOWN never contributes negative evidence and is never silently treated as agreement.
09
Cross-encoder rerank
DITTO-style: [CLS] [COL] attr [VAL] val … [SEP] … with span tags [THREAD] [LEN] [GRADE]. This is your reranker — not a separate model from "the DITTO model". Cached in match_candidate keyed by (src, dst, model_version).
10
VETO GATE
Any DISAGREE on a CRITICAL attribute → score is forced to zero. Not a penalty. Not low confidence. Rejected, with the vetoing attribute named. Dimension mismatch (50 mm vs 50 kg) is also a veto — Pint raises on incompatible units for free.
11
Score & calibrate
Logistic regression (~15 features) over neural score, attribute agreement, lexical similarity, unknown-counts. Temperature-scale the neural head; Platt or isotonic on the ensemble depending on calibration-set size.
12
Decide with abstention
Two thresholds, three zones. Auto-approve band chosen by target precision with a Clopper–Pearson lower bound, not by F1. Class-conditional split conformal for the abstention set.
13
Explain
The attribute table is computed, always. LLM narration optional, local, and never on the decision path.
14
Human review → unified master
Append-only decision log capturing model version, gazetteer version, tolerance-policy version and thresholds. A decision must be replayable in 2029.

The scoring function

# Identity is decided by structure. Similarity only proposes candidates.

gates    = [g(a) for a in attrs if a.criticality == CRITICAL]
           # g(a) = 1 if AGREE or UNKNOWN, 0 if DISAGREE
veto     = min(gates)                    # 0 kills the pair outright

s_neural = cross_encoder(serialised_pair)          # [0,1]
s_attr   = weighted_agreement(IMPORTANT, MINOR)    # [0,1]
s_lex    = normalised_lexical_similarity           # [0,1]

s_raw    = calibrator([s_neural, s_attr, s_lex,
                       n_unknown, n_critical_known, ...])

score    = veto * s_raw
klass    = decide(score, veto, disagreeing_attributes)
Why a gate and not a weight

A weighted blend can always be overwhelmed. If the cosine similarity between SS HEX BOLT M10 X 50 and SS HEX BOLT M10 X 75 is 0.985 — and it will be, because for natural language those strings are nearly identical — then any weighting scheme that lets a strong text signal outvote a length mismatch will eventually merge them. The embedding model is not broken; it is correctly doing a job that is the wrong job.

Under a gate, that merge is not unlikely. It is impossible. That is the difference between a system a refinery could deploy and a system that looks good in a slide.

Handling the hard cases

CaseTreatment
Missing attributeUNKNOWN. Contributes zero evidence — neither confirms nor denies. Widens the confidence interval without moving the point estimate. This is the Fellegi–Sunter (1969) treatment of missing fields, and citing that lineage reads as competence.
Conflicting attributeDISAGREE. If CRITICAL → veto. If MINOR → confidence penalty only. Conflating this with UNKNOWN is the single most common design error here.
Numeric tolerancePer-attribute, versioned, with recorded provenance. Thread diameter: tol=0, because thread sizes are discrete not continuous. Length: tol_abs=0.5mm. Weight: tol_rel=0.05. Never invent a global ±5%. Where unknown, set 0 and let a human widen it — a false negative goes to a reviewer, a false positive corrupts the national master.
Unit inferenceM10 X 50 has no units. Infer mm from the metric thread prefix, and surface the inference: length: 50 (unit inferred: mm, rule THREAD_METRIC_IMPLIES_MM).
Pack-size unitsEA/NO/PC/NOS/U are synonyms for "each". SET/PR/BOX-100/RL are quantities of an item. Two records differing only in EA vs BOX-100 are the same material — a duplicate class your brief's framing would miss.
AbbreviationsLearned, not hand-written: LLM proposes candidates from 5,000 distinct descriptions offline, a human accepts/rejects, the result is a versioned dictionary. FLIS ships V_COLLOQUIAL_NAME — a real curated alias dictionary — for free.
MultilingualCut for v1 pending verification. Handle transliteration noise and Indian-English abbreviations instead. Revisit only if a domain contact confirms non-English descriptions exist.

Turn your biggest weakness into your best slide

In petroleum refining, declaring two items interchangeable has statutory consequences: pressure class (ASME B16.5), material certification (NACE MR0175 for sour service), hazardous-area certification (ATEX/IECEx/PESO), fire-safe design, OEM warranty. A system that autonomously asserts functional equivalence is a liability, and a CPCL judge will spot it in seconds.

So make the refusal a feature you demo on purpose. Show the system finding a genuine equivalence candidate, and show it declining to act: "the AI is deliberately not permitted to make this call — it routes to a materials engineer." That single design decision converts your most attackable class into your most mature-sounding moment.

Synthetic data — the rule that makes it rigorous

Real identity, synthetic surface

Indefensible: invent items, invent which pairs match, train, report 97% F1. The model has learned only your generator, and a competent evaluator will say so.

Defensible: take a real canonical item with a real code, real classification and real attribute values; apply a surface transformation that provably preserves identity. The pair is positive because the underlying entity is the same.

And never let one generator produce both training and test data. Train on rule-based generator G1; test on a deliberately different generator G2 (an LLM asked to rewrite as an Indian storekeeper would), with entity-disjoint items.

The shortcut failure mode — guard against it explicitly

If all your positives come from augmentation and all your negatives from spec mutation, the model can learn the artefacts of your generator rather than the semantics — e.g. "records differing by exactly one integer are negatives." It will score beautifully and be useless.

Two guards, both mandatory: (a) a held-out set of hand-labelled real pairs; (b) an ablation that tests on real pairs only. Mix ~50% constructed negatives with ~50% mined-from-corpus negatives so the model also learns lexical confusables you did not think of.

Splits, and the leakage trap

Split by entity, never by pair

This is the single most common way student ML projects produce fake numbers. Hold out entire canonical materials. Make it a code-review checklist item, and run the pair-level-split ablation deliberately so you can show the inflation it causes.

Why fine-tuning is not where the value is

DITTO's gains over the prior state of the art are +27 to +32 F1 on Dirty and Textual benchmarks but only ~+5 on Structured. After your extraction stage runs, your problem is a Structured problem. Expect single-digit gains from a cross-encoder over a well-engineered feature model — not the 30-point miracle the headline suggests.

The team's instinct will be to spend weeks on training tricks. That instinct is wrong here. Your bottleneck is labelled evaluation data and the extraction stage. Allocate accordingly.

Calibration recipe

  1. Hold out a calibration set sampled the way production data arrives — random over records, not over pairs, and not balanced. A 50/50 set will lie to you about production precision. Log the prevalence.
  2. Temperature-scale the neural head (ER-specific evidence: most effective method, ECE reduced up to 23.8%). Platt on the ensemble if n<1000, isotonic if ≥1000.
  3. Verify on a separate test set: reliability diagram, ECE, MCE, Brier score. Put the reliability diagram in the deck — almost nobody does, and it is instantly legible as rigour.
  4. Pick the auto-approve threshold by target precision with a Clopper–Pearson lower bound, not by F1: "at threshold t, precision ≥ 99% with 95% confidence, n = N pairs above threshold, covering X% of true duplicates."
  5. Two thresholds, three zones. Report the review rate.
This reframes your entire evaluation story

"We auto-decide 71% of pairs at ≥99% precision and route 29% to a human" is a far more compelling product claim than any F1 — and it is the framing a government buyer actually cares about. It turns human-in-the-loop from an admission of weakness into the core value proposition: how much expert time the platform saves at a guaranteed error rate. Build the deck around this sentence.

Design the unmap path. Nobody else will.

A CPSE must be able to dispute and unmap. It is the first thing a materials manager will ask about, and every competing student project will have a one-way merge. Mappings are versioned and superseded, never deleted. Merge and split of canonical materials must both exist — split is the part everyone forgets, and it is needed the moment a reviewer discovers one NMC actually covers two distinct parts.

Do not design a clever national code

Resist the urge to build a 14-digit semantic code where digits encode group / subgroup / dimension. Significant codes break when an item is reclassified, run out of ranges, and encode a taxonomy that will be wrong in five years. The NATO Stock Number gets this right: only the first four digits classify, and the identity itself (the NIIN) is non-significant. MESC — the 10-digit hierarchical scheme used in oil & gas, proprietary to Shell — is prior art to cite, not to copy. Spending a week on code format is a week on the least valuable artefact in the project.

Reviewer throughput is a genuine differentiator

A real reviewer must process hundreds of decisions per hour. Every competing demo will review one item at a time with a mouse. Design for volume and say why:

  • Keyboard-driven review — 1/2/3/4 to decide, j/k to move, u to undo. No mouse in the happy path.
  • Bulk-approve above a confidence threshold, with mandatory sampling audit — approve 2,000 pairs at ≥0.99, and the system forces you to review a random 2% sample first and reports the observed error rate.
  • Queue prioritisation — by confidence band, by category fragmentation, by number of CPSEs affected.
  • Undo everywhere, because a reviewer moving at speed will misfire.

Then put the number on a slide: "a reviewer clears N decisions per hour at a measured error rate of X%." That is a product metric, not a model metric, and it is what a buyer procures.

2 · The evidence panel

Not "MATCH = 97.4%". A computed table: attribute, source value, candidate value, verdict, plus the raw and canonicalised forms, the extraction provenance for each value, and — when the veto fires — the attribute that fired it stated in plain language. The score is the last row, not the headline.

Hard rule: every number in this panel comes from computation. An LLM may rephrase the table into prose displayed beside it; it may never produce a number. A judge who probes this will find the boundary holds.

The blockchain question — you asked, here is the straight answer

No. And the refusal is worth more than the implementation.

A blockchain solves Byzantine agreement among mutually distrusting parties with no common authority. CPSEs are all under a common ministry. There is a trusted authority. The problem the technology solves does not exist here. A permissioned chain run by one operator is a hash-chained append-only log with extra containers and a consensus protocol you do not need.

It costs weeks and a new class of demo-day failure. It buys one slide bullet. And it is a liability: when a strong judge asks "what is your consensus mechanism and who are the validators?", the honest answer exposes it as decorative.

Raise and dismiss it yourself, on a slide: "We evaluated blockchain for audit integrity and rejected it. CPSEs share a trust anchor, so Byzantine consensus is unnecessary. We implement hash-chained append-only audit with external anchoring — equivalent tamper-evidence at a fraction of the operational cost." That slide will impress a serious judge more than an actual blockchain would, because it demonstrates engineering judgment, which is the rarest thing in the room.

Be precise about what hash chaining proves

It gives tamper-evidence against a party who can modify rows but cannot recompute and republish the whole chain. An attacker with full database write access can rewrite every subsequent hash. The chain becomes meaningful only when the head hash is periodically anchored somewhere the DBA does not control — a daily signed compliance report, an email to an auditor, or WORM object storage. Evaluation strategy

This is what converts "we built an AI" into "we proved it works" — and it is the cheapest credibility in the entire project. One day of benchmark work buys more than any UI feature.

Metrics, and which ones are honest

MetricWhat it measures here

Per-attribute extraction P/R/F1Measured on ~500 hand-labelled descriptions. Turns error analysis from guessing into engineering.

The base-rate trap — the fastest way to a fake number

In a real corpus, duplicates are rare. A test set balanced 50/50 will wildly overstate precision, because precision depends on prevalence. If you report 0.96 precision on a balanced set and the true duplicate rate is 2%, your production precision could be under 0.4.

Report precision at realistic prevalence, log the prevalence of every set you evaluate on, and show the two numbers side by side. A judge who knows statistics will ask; a judge who does not will still be impressed that you volunteered it.

The asymmetric cost argument

Deliver this argument well and it outweighs any dashboard

Wrongly merging two different materials in a procurement system can cause the wrong part to be issued to a live refinery — a safety and operational event. Wrongly failing to merge costs some inefficiency and a reviewer's time. These costs are not symmetric, so F1 — which weights precision and recall equally — is the wrong objective.

Use F-beta with β < 1 (β = 0.5 weights precision twice as heavily), or an explicit cost matrix. State the operating point: target ≥99% precision on auto-approved merges, accept whatever recall follows, and route the remainder to human review. Then report the coverage that policy achieves.

Hostile questions, with answers ready

Isn't this just embeddings with extra steps?

No — and here is the ablation that proves it. Remove the attribute veto and precision on our hard slice falls from A to B while overall F1 barely moves, because hard negatives are rare in a random sample and dominant in production. Embeddings decide candidacy. Structure decides identity. Here is a pair with 0.98 cosine similarity that we reject.

Where did your labels come from?

From V_FLIS_PART — many manufacturer part numbers recorded against one canonical stock number by a national codification authority — and from V_FLIS_STANDARDIZATION, which records actual human standardization decisions. We did not assert a single label ourselves, and we did not use an LLM to generate any.

Your training data is synthetic. Doesn't that make the results meaningless?

The identities are real; only the surface text is synthetic. We separate them in reporting: Test B is 100% real with zero synthesis, Test C is real Indian tender lines we collected by hand, Test D is four published academic benchmarks with other people's numbers to compare against. Our headline is the real number, not the synthetic one.

Verdantis and Prospecta already sell this. Why you?

We do not claim to beat them on matching quality — we cannot measure that and it is probably false; their moat is a proprietary OEM part reference database. We do something they do not: mint a shared national identity across independently governed enterprises with a central adjudicating authority and per-CPSE sovereignty over existing codes. Every commercial tool cleanses one enterprise's master. Ours is cross-organisational, licence-clean, self-hostable and air-gap capable.

Why not just use ChatGPT to compare the two descriptions?

We benchmarked it — baseline B7. It is slower, uncalibrated, non-reproducible across runs, and it has the same numeric weakness with more convincing prose. A government audit trail must give the same answer in 2029 as in 2026. And at 1M records × 20 candidates that is 20M calls per pass, which is not a national-scale architecture at any price.

What about valves, motors, instruments — not just bolts?

Six commodity families of graded difficulty, and we will demo two live. Fasteners establish the mechanism; valves show multi-attribute high-consequence matching; bearings show reference-method matching where descriptions share almost no words.

Can the AI decide two materials are interchangeable?

Deliberately not. In refining, interchangeability has statutory consequences — pressure class, NACE certification for sour service, hazardous-area certification. The system records expert-asserted equivalences and surfaces candidates, but it will not auto-approve one at any confidence level. We will show you it refusing.

How do we know you didn't leak test data into training?

Splits are entity-level, not pair-level — no canonical material appears on both sides. We also ran the pair-level split deliberately as an ablation so we could measure the inflation it causes: it moves F1 by Δ, which is why our number is lower than it could have been.

Recommended default: assume Kaggle free tier and design so fine-tuning is an upgrade, not a dependency. An off-the-shelf embedding model plus a well-engineered feature scorer already gets most of the way. If you have a college lab GPU, say so and we widen the experiment budget.

Faculty, alumni, family. Not for data — for 15 minutes of sanity-checking item names and settling whether descriptions are ever non-English. This is the highest-leverage half-hour available to you and it costs nothing.

4 · Which six commodity families?

Recommended: fasteners, pipes/flanges/gaskets, valves, bearings, motors & cables, lubricants. Valves and bearings are non-negotiable — valves because CPCL judges care, bearings because they showcase reference-method matching. Swap the others freely.

Our answer: it is mandatory. Without ~300–500 hand-labelled real pairs you have no honest evaluation number, and every metric you report is measuring your own generator.
